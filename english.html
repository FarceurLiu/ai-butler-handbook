Want AI to help, but unsure where to start?

The missing piece may not be another tool. This handbook uses worked examples, task templates, and checklists to help with three common difficulties.

What can I assign?
Start with a low-risk task you can check yourself, such as a summary, a task list, or a first draft.
Why is the result not useful?
Make the goal, source material, limits, and output format clear. Use the first draft to see what needs correction.
Can I trust the answer?
Compare the result with its sources. Separate supported information from assumptions and open questions; keep judgment and responsibility with you.
Practice with the handbook

Try the example. Then use your own material.

Try the first task in Chapter 2

  1. Explain the task

    Choose a low-risk task whose result you can check. Provide the goal, source material, limits, and output format.

  2. Check and refine the result

    Compare it with the source. Look for omissions, misunderstandings, and invented details. Correct problems and confirm missing information.

  3. Save what worked

    After checking the result, keep useful instructions and checks as a template you can adapt for similar tasks.

Take four practical skills into your next task

The meeting notes are a starting point. Use the same habits when you summarize material, draft a reply, or organize a new task.

Assign clearly
Give AI the material and instructions it needs to begin, rather than a vague request to handle everything.
Judge the result
Distinguish supported information from open questions. Know what needs checking and when to stop and ask a person.
Keep useful methods
Build your own task templates and checklists, so similar work starts with a method you can adapt.
Choose tools
Recognize when your current tool is enough, and when you need file handling, reusable instructions, or an assistant that can carry a task forward.
